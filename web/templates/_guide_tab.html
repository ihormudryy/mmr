Dashboard guide

This page explains every part of the dashboard in plain language, with step-by-step examples. It assumes no trading experience. Start here — what this dashboard does {{ info('Five minutes here saves an hour of confusion later. Everything else in this guide builds on these two ideas: the trade lifecycle, and paper vs live.') }}

This is the control room for a brokerage account at Interactive Brokers (IB). The one rule that makes it safe: nothing is ever bought or sold until you press Approve.

Every trade follows the same five steps:

  1. An idea appears. You have one, a strategy (an automatic rule) spots one, or the Research tab finds one.
  2. The idea becomes a proposal. A proposal is a written request: “buy this, roughly this much, because of this.” It is not a trade. It sits in the Action queue waiting for you.
  3. You decide. Read the card. Approve sends it to the broker; Reject throws it away. Ignored proposals quietly expire after about 30 minutes.
  4. The broker executes. An approved proposal becomes an order. When someone on the market takes the other side, the order fills — that is the actual trade.
  5. You now hold a position. Its value moves with the price. Later you close it (sell what you bought), and the profit or loss becomes final.

Paper vs Live {{ info('The badge is always visible in the command band under the tabs. On LIVE the whole band is tinted red so you can never mistake which account you are touching.') }}

BadgeWhat it meansAttitude
PAPER A practice account with pretend money. Real prices, real mechanics, zero risk. Experiment freely. Mistakes cost nothing.
LIVE Real money. Every risk-increasing action asks you to confirm a second time. Slow down. Read every confirm dialog fully.

Stay on paper until the whole loop — propose, approve, watch the fill, close the position — feels boring. Boring means you understand it.

Trading tab — the main screen {{ info('Laid out action-first: the decisions waiting for you sit at the top, your holdings in the middle, and the supporting panels underneath.') }}

Action queue {{ info('Filters: Pending shows cards awaiting a decision (the count is in the button). All adds everything. History shows finished proposals — executed, rejected, expired, failed.') }}

Every proposal is a card: side (BUY/SELL), symbol, age, quantity (or “auto”), notional value, confidence, status, source (who proposed it), and the thesis. On a PENDING card: Approve sends it to the broker, Reject discards it, Details opens the full record. On LIVE, Approve additionally opens a red confirm dialog restating side, quantity, and value — read it, then confirm.

Positions {{ info('Last price and its age come from live quotes. A row with a red tint and a ⚠ next to the age means the quote is stale — the shown price may be out of date; do not make decisions from it.') }}

Everything you currently hold: quantity, average cost, last price, market value, unrealized P&L, day P&L, and quote freshness. Close on a row opens a pre-filled exit proposal — lower the quantity to close only part. It still needs your Approve; nothing sells on the click itself.

Working orders {{ info('Orders are grouped: an entry with its protective stop/take-profit legs shows as one family. The tags tell you which is which.') }}

Orders sitting at the broker, not yet filled. Each leg is tagged ENTRY (would open new risk) or PROTECTIVE (guards an existing position). Cancel removes one leg; Cancel all (top right, with a strong confirm) removes every working order — including protective stops, which leaves positions unguarded. Use Cancel all only when you understand exactly why.

Recent executions

The receipt tape: time, instrument, side, quantity, price, commission for every actual fill.

Strategies (runtime panel) {{ info('This panel controls the running instances. Deploying new strategies or removing them happens on the Strategies tab; this is the on/off and tuning surface.') }}

Live strategy rows with state chips — RUNNING (working normally), ERROR (something failed; the note column says what). Enable / Disable switch a strategy on or off immediately. Edit params opens a dialog to adjust its tunable settings on the fly (takes effect without a restart).

Risk & reconciliation {{ info('“Reconciliation” means comparing the dashboard’s records with what the broker says you actually hold, and flagging any mismatch — e.g. a position whose protective stop has disappeared.') }}

Warnings about the shape of the whole account: concentration, group budgets over their limit, positions without protection, record mismatches. “Risk unavailable” is not good news — it means the risk check cannot run, not that everything is fine.

Example A — your first paper trade {{ info('The best first exercise. Total cost if it goes badly: pretend money.') }}

  1. Check the command band says PAPER.
  2. Click New proposal. In the drawer, type AAPL in Symbol and click Resolve — the exact conId fills itself in. (For a non-US stock, set Exchange and Currency first, e.g. ASX / AUD.)
  3. Side BUY. Enter a small Notional amount, e.g. 500 — that means “buy about $500 worth”. (You can instead leave quantity and amount empty to let the server size it from your confidence; if sizing is not configured it answers SIZING_BLOCKED — just set an amount.)
  4. Confidence 0.6, and one honest line of reasoning — future-you will read it.
  5. Create proposal. The card appears in the Action queue.
  6. Read your own card as if a stranger wrote it. Then Approve.
  7. Watch it flow: Working orders (briefly) → Recent executions (the fill) → Positions (your new holding).

Change of heart at step 6? Reject. Nothing was sent; nothing is lost.

Example B — close some or all of it

  1. Positions → the AAPL row → Close.
  2. The drawer is pre-filled with the full quantity — lower it to close only part.
  3. Create → the exit card appears in the queue → Approve.
  4. After the fill, the position shrinks or disappears, and the result becomes realized P&L.

Example C — something feels wrong

  1. Command band → Pause new trading. Instant; blocks all new risk.
  2. Reject any pending proposals you do not fully understand.
  3. Investigate calmly (health chips, Risk panel, Strategies panel).
  4. Resume when satisfied.

Example D — cancel an order without hurting yourself

  1. Working orders → find the leg → look at its tag first.
  2. ENTRY: cancelling just abandons a pending trade. Safe.
  3. PROTECTIVE: cancelling removes a stop-loss or take-profit — the position it guarded is then unprotected. Only do this deliberately (e.g. right before closing the position itself).

Example E — from scan to proposal

  1. Research → Ideas → preset momentum → Run Ideas.
  2. Click a row that looks interesting; read its metrics and headlines in the detail panel.
  3. Click Propose on the detail — the New proposal drawer opens pre-filled: resolved conId, side, confidence from the score, thesis and reasoning from the row (group tag research).
  4. Adjust the size, then Create — and decide on the card in the Action queue like any other proposal.

Strategies tab — deploying automatic rules {{ info('Deploying means: write the strategy into the runtime config attached to your chosen symbols, load it immediately, and enable it. The strategy service also re-reads config within ~30 seconds by itself.') }}

Two tables:

  • Deployed strategies — what is loaded right now: state (RUNNING / DISABLED / ERROR), whether it proposes (the propose chip), bar size, and the symbols or portfolio it watches. Actions: Enable / Disable / Undeploy (undeploy removes it from config, with a confirm).
  • Available strategies — every strategy file found on disk, with its description, tunable parameters and their defaults. ▸ deploy unfolds the deploy form.

The deploy form needs symbols or a portfolio — exactly one, not both, not neither. Bar size is how often the strategy thinks: a “5 mins” strategy re-evaluates once per completed 5-minute price bar. Tick propose signals so its ideas appear as cards in your Action queue — otherwise signals are only recorded for study.

Example F — deploy one strategy on two stocks

  1. Portfolios tab: create tech_demo, add AAPL, MSFT (Example G shows how).
  2. Strategies tab → Available strategies → pick one → ▸ deploy.
  3. Name it (e.g. orb_demo), choose portfolio tech_demo, keep the default bar size and parameters.
  4. Tick propose signals → Deploy & enable.
  5. Watch the Trading tab’s Strategies panel: RUNNING. When its rule fires, a card appears in your Action queue — approve or reject it like any other.

Portfolios tab — reusable symbol lists {{ info('Stored as universes in the local database. Every symbol is resolved to an exact IB contract when added — never guessed from the ticker letters.') }}

Create a list once, then point strategies at it. Click a portfolio’s name to unfold its member panel: tick-and-remove members, add symbols, upload a CSV (a symbol column, optionally exchange and currency), or delete the whole list.

Example G — an Australian portfolio (why exchange & currency matter)

  1. Create asx_miners (names: letters, digits, dash, underscore).
  2. Unfold it → add symbols BHP, RIO with exchange ASX and currency AUD.
  3. Result: the real Australian listings. Without the hints you could silently get a US-listed instrument that happens to share the ticker letters.

Scaling tab — advanced controls {{ info('Nothing here is needed for everyday propose-and-approve trading. Come back after the learning path at the bottom of this guide.') }}

Allocation & scaling (live capital ceilings) {{ info('The signing dance exists so that raising real-money limits requires a private key that never touches this browser. The dashboard only builds and accepts JSON documents.') }}

A signed permission slip that caps how much real capital one strategy may use. Flow: fill the form → Generate unsigned JSON → sign it offline with the CLI (mmr research allocation sign …) → paste the signed version → Activate. Suspend (with a reason) immediately revokes an active authority — it is risk-reducing and always available. The Authority history table below records every change.

Paper automation {{ info('The checklist above the controls must be green first — command authority enabled, paper account, artifacts writable. Expect lifecycle “armed” after Activate; “armed_unpersisted” means click Activate once more to finish saving; “failed” means read the error.') }}

The one sanctioned exception to approve-first: exactly one chosen strategy is armed to place its own protected orders (entry plus stop-loss) without a human click — paper accounts only; the server refuses to arm this on live. Pick the strategy, write a reason, Activate; Deactivate stands it down instantly.

Kill switches, strongest first: Deactivate paper automation (Scaling) · Pause new trading (command band) · disable the strategy (Trading → Strategies panel). All are immediate.

When the system says no — refusal codes {{ info('These are guardrails, not errors. Each refusal is one specific safety rule that fired. The code appears in the toast message or on the proposal card.') }}

CodeIn plain wordsWhat to do
SIZING_BLOCKEDYou left quantity and amount empty, and automatic sizing is not configured on this server.Enter a quantity or a notional amount.
QUOTE_UNAVAILABLENo usable current price for this instrument (market data missing).Check market hours and data subscriptions; try again.
QUOTE_STALEThe freshest available price is too old to trade on safely.Wait for live data; typical outside market hours.
PRICE_DRIFT_EXCEEDEDThe price has moved too far from what the proposal card showed — approving now would fill far from what you read.Reject and re-propose at current prices if still interested.
ORDER_NOTIONAL_LIMITThe order is bigger than the per-order size ceiling configured on the server.Propose a smaller size (or raise the configured ceiling deliberately).
TRADING_PAUSEDPause new trading is on; new risk is blocked.Resume first — or enjoy the pause you asked for.
TRADING_FILTER_REJECTEDThis instrument is on a deny list (or missing from the allow list).Intentional guardrail; pick another instrument.

Is everything healthy? {{ info('Rule of thumb: green chips + advancing “last event” + no banners = trust the screen. Anything else: verify before acting.') }}

  • Health chips (command band): all green dots = all systems connected. A red chip names the broken dependency.
  • last event (tab row): should keep advancing during market hours.
  • Stale position rows (red tint + ⚠ age): that price is old — do not act on it.
  • “Risk unavailable” in the Risk panel means the check cannot run — never read it as “no problems”.
  • Missing data is a warning, not an all-clear. When in doubt: Pause, investigate, Resume.

Suggested learning path

  1. Confirm the badge says PAPER.
  2. Skim “The words, organized” above once.
  3. Example A — first manual trade; Example B — close it.
  4. Example C — practice Pause / Resume once, calmly, so it is familiar before you ever need it.
  5. Example G then F — build a small portfolio, deploy one strategy with propose.
  6. Approve or reject a few strategy proposals; read every card properly.
  7. Example E — drive one proposal from Research.
  8. Only then consider LIVE — and treat every red confirm dialog as real money, because it is.

Quick tips

  • Always Resolve in the proposal drawer — the conId is the exact contract; ticker letters alone can be ambiguous.
  • Any drawer closes without sending: Cancel, ×, or Escape.
  • Pause new trading and New proposal live in the command band, on every tab.
  • Check the ENTRY / PROTECTIVE tag before cancelling any order.
  • Write reasoning you would understand three weeks later.
  • A refusal code is the system protecting you — read it, don’t fight it.
  • Confused? Pause → reject what you don’t understand → investigate → Resume.
